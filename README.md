# An Exploration of the Monster Group in the Brauer Atlas

This document provides a summary of information gathered from the Atlas of Finite Group Representations, with a specific focus on the Monster group (M) and one of its permutation representations. The information is based on the data available in Version 3 of the Atlas hosted at Queen Mary University of London [1].

## The Atlas of Finite Group Representations

The Atlas of Finite Group Representations is a comprehensive online database containing information about the representations of finite simple groups [1]. It is a valuable resource for mathematicians and researchers working in group theory. The Atlas is available in multiple versions, with Version 3 being the most recent stable release, though it is no longer actively maintained.

## The Monster Group (M)

The Monster group (M) is the largest of the 26 sporadic simple groups and is notable for its immense size and complex structure. The Atlas provides a wealth of information about the Monster [2].

### Key Properties

The fundamental properties of the Monster group are summarized in the table below:

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| **Order** | 808,017,424,794,512,875,886,459,904,961,710,757,005,754,368,000,000,000 |
| **Prime Factorization** | 2⁴⁶ · 3²⁰ · 5⁹ · 7⁶ · 11² · 13³ · 17 · 19 · 23 · 29 · 31 · 41 · 47 · 59 · 71 |
| **Schur Multiplier** | 1 (Trivial) |
| **Outer Automorphism Group** | 1 (Trivial) |

### Standard Generators

The Monster group can be generated by two elements, **a** and **b**, with the following properties:

- **a** belongs to the conjugacy class 2A.
- **b** belongs to the conjugacy class 3B.
- The product **ab** has an order of 29.

### Maximal Subgroups and Conjugacy Classes

The Monster group has a rich and complex substructure, characterized by its maximal subgroups and conjugacy classes.

- **Maximal Subgroups**: The Monster has 44 conjugacy classes of maximal subgroups. These subgroups are themselves large and complex groups, such as the Baby Monster group (2.B) and the Fischer group (3.Fi₂₄").
- **Conjugacy Classes**: There are 194 distinct conjugacy classes in the Monster group. These classes are labeled from 1A to 119B, indicating the order of the elements in the class.

## Permutation Representation of a Maximal Subgroup

The Atlas also contains detailed information about the representations of the maximal subgroups of the Monster. One such example is the permutation representation for the 41st maximal subgroup, 7²:SL₂(7) [3].

### Representation Details

This specific representation, identified as `Mmax41G0-p49B0`, has the following characteristics:

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| **Group** | 7²:SL₂(7) |
| **Number of Points** | 49 |
| **Primitivity** | Primitive |

This representation describes how the maximal subgroup 7²:SL₂(7) acts on a set of 49 points. The data for this representation is available for download in various formats, including MeatAxe, GAP, and Magma, which are computational algebra systems used for group theory research.
